    Method & assumptions

    How the regime is decided

    The transition from dripping to jetting is governed by two forces overcoming interfacial tension: viscous shear from the continuous phase (capillary number Cac = μcUc/γ) and inertia of the dispersed phase (Weber number Wed = ρdUd²L/γ). Following Utada et al. (2007), the transition is approximated as S = Cac/Ca* + Wed/We* ≈ 1, where the critical values Ca* and We* depend on the geometry of the microfluidic device (listed below): S < 0.7 → dripping, 0.7–1.3 → transitional, > 1.3 → jetting. Jets driven mainly by Ca are narrowing jets; jets driven mainly by We are widening jets.

    • Co-flow: Ca* ≈ 1, We* ≈ 1 (Utada et al., PRL 99, 094502, 2007).
    • Flow-focusing: Ca* ≈ 0.2, We* ≈ 1 (Cubaud & Mason, Phys. Fluids 20, 053302, 2008). Cac < 0.01 is labelled squeezing.
    • T-junction: Ca* ≈ 0.1, We* ≈ 1; squeezing below Cac ≈ 0.01 (Garstecki et al., Lab Chip 6, 437, 2006). Qd/Qc > 4 with Cac > 0.01 is flagged as parallel (stratified) flow.
    How droplet size is estimated

    The size is found in two steps: a size is calculated for pure dripping (a formula specific to each geometry) and for pure jetting, and the transition parameter S then selects or blends them.

    1. Dripping size, by geometry

    • T-junction, squeezing (Cac < 0.01) (Garstecki et al. 2006): the forming drop blocks the channel and is pinched off by the pressure building up behind it. Plug length L/w = 1 + Qd/Qc. The plug volume is V ≈ w·h·(L − w/4) (w/4 allows for the rounded ends) and the reported size is the equivalent-sphere diameter d = (6V/π)1/3.
    • T-junction, dripping (Cac ≥ 0.01): shear pinches the drop off earlier, so the size shrinks as d ∝ Cac−1/4, starting from the squeezing value at Cac = 0.01. Published exponents are roughly −0.2 to −0.25.
    • Flow-focusing: d/w ≈ 0.9 · (Qd/Qc)0.3 · Cac−0.2, with Qc counting both side channels. More dispersed flow gives larger drops; more shear gives smaller ones. The trends and approximate exponents match published flow-focusing studies, but the constants 0.9, 0.3 and −0.2 are typical values chosen for this app, not taken from one paper. This is the formula that most needs calibrating against your device.
    • Co-flow (Umbanhowar et al. 2000): the drop stays on the tip until the outer fluid's drag beats the interfacial tension holding it, 3π·μc·(d − dtip)·(Uc − Ud) ≈ π·dtip·γ, which gives d ≈ dtip·(1 + 1/(3Ca′)) with Ca′ = μc(Uc − Ud)/γ. The slip velocity is kept at no less than 5% of Uc, and the size is capped at the outer tube diameter D.

    2. Jetting size (all geometries)

    • Jet diameter from mass conservation: assuming both phases move at the same speed in the outlet (plug flow), the jet takes up the same share of the outlet area as its share of the total flow, djet = √((4/π)·Aout·Qd/(Qd + Qc)).
    • Rayleigh–Plateau breakup: a thread breaks up fastest for disturbances with wavelength λ ≈ 4.51·djet, and each wavelength becomes one drop. Equating volumes, (π/4)·djet²·4.51·djet = (π/6)·d³, gives d ≈ 1.89·djet.

    3. Combining them with S

    • S < 0.7: dripping size. S > 1.3: jetting size.
    • In between, the two are blended in log scale: with t = (S − 0.7)/0.6, ln d = (1 − t)·ln ddrip + t·ln djet drop. This avoids a sudden jump in size at the boundary.

    4. Quantities derived from the size

    • Generation rate: f = Qd / (π d³/6), the dispersed flow rate divided by the volume of one drop.
    • Confinement: drops larger than the smallest channel dimension (or about D for co-flow) are flagged as plugs. The size shown is then the equivalent-sphere diameter, not what a top-down image would show.
    • Parallel flow (T-junction): a size is still calculated, but no drops actually form in this regime.

    How far to trust the size

    SituationConfidence
    T-junction squeezingGood: a well-tested scaling, typically within about 10–20%
    Co-flow drippingModerate: a simplified force balance
    JettingModerate: plug flow is assumed, but in real channels the centre moves faster, giving thinner jets and smaller drops. Very viscous threads break at longer wavelengths (Tomotika), giving larger drops than 1.89·djet.
    Flow-focusing drippingRough: trends are right, but the constants need calibrating
    Transitional bandRough: the blend is a smooth guess, not physics

    The sizes are best used for trends and order of magnitude. Measured drop sizes from your own device are the way to pin down the constants.

    How dispersity is estimated

    Dispersity is reported as the coefficient of variation CV = σ/d̄ and PDI = CV². These are heuristic values from typical experimental ranges, not first-principles predictions: squeezing ≈ 1.5%, dripping ≈ 2.5%, narrowing jets ≈ 8%, widening (inertial) jets ≈ 20%, rising smoothly through the transitional band. Extreme viscosity ratios (λ < 0.05 or > 20) and strong inertia (Rec > 50) add spread. Satellite droplets are flagged when jetting with Ohnesorge number Ohd < 0.1. CV < 5% is called monodisperse, 5–10% moderately monodisperse, > 10% polydisperse.
